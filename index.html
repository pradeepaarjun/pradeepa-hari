Stay connected & pay easily in India

A few useful tips to help you stay connected and make everyday payments during your trip.

💳

Paying with UPI

UPI is used everywhere in India — at restaurants, shops, taxis and many small local businesses.

Don't have an Indian bank account? No problem! International visitors can use UPI One World, a prepaid UPI wallet designed for travellers.

  1. Download a supported UPI One World app.
  2. Complete the required verification.
  3. Add money using your international debit/credit card.
  4. Scan the merchant's UPI QR code.
  5. Pay directly in Indian Rupees (₹).
💡 Our tip: Keep your French bank card and some Indian cash as a backup.
🌐 Learn more about UPI One World
📱

Getting a SIM card

An Indian SIM can make your trip much easier for mobile data, Google Maps, WhatsApp and local calls.

The main mobile operators are Jio, Airtel and Vi.

What to bring

  • ☑️ Passport
  • ☑️ Valid Indian visa / immigration document
  • ☑️ Your accommodation address in India
  • ☑️ Your phone
💡 Our tip: Buy your SIM from an official operator store or authorised retailer. If your phone supports eSIM, you can also check whether an eSIM option is available.

📲 Keep your French number

If your phone supports dual SIM, you can keep your French SIM and use an Indian SIM at the same time.

🇫🇷 French SIM Keep your French number for SMS, banking and important messages.
🇮🇳 Indian SIM Use it for mobile data, Indian calls and everyday travel.

A little guide to India

A few things that may be different from France.

🍛

Food

Discover delicious South Indian dishes such as dosa, idli, sambar, parotta and South Indian thali. If you'd like to order food to your hotel, you can use apps such as Zomato, Swiggy or Uber Eats. For groceries and everyday essentials, apps like Blinkit and Instamart offer convenient home delivery.

👗

What to Wear

  • Light, breathable fabrics (cotton, linen)
  • One festive/formal outfit for the reception
  • Comfortable shoes that slip on/off easily (temples)
  • A scarf or shawl for temple visits
  • Basic medicine kit, sunscreen, mosquito repellent
  • Universal power adapter (India uses Type C/D/M, 230V)
🙏

Culture

Learn a few simple customs before visiting temples and homes.

☀️

Weather

Madurai in late January is warm and dry — expect roughly 21°C at night to 32°C in the afternoon. Mornings can feel pleasantly cool, especially for the 9:30 AM ceremony.

🗣️

Tamil

Learn a few words to impress our families!

👍 Do

  • Be curious and open-minded.
  • Try the local food.
  • Respect religious places.
  • Dress modestly at temples — shoulders and knees covered.
  • Remove your shoes before entering temples and many homes.
  • Use your right hand for eating, giving, or receiving items.
  • Greet people with hands pressed together — "Vanakkam" in Tamil.
  • Drink bottled or filtered water only.
  • Ask permission before photographing people, especially inside temples.
  • Ease into spicy food gradually and carry basic stomach medicine.

👎 Don't

  • Don't offer or accept things with your left hand where possible.
  • Don't enter restricted temple areas.
  • Don't make jokes about religious traditions.
  • Don't panic when you see Indian traffic! 😄
  • Don't be alarmed by traffic and honking — it's normal. Be especially careful when crossing roads and use pedestrian crossings when available.
  • Don't wear shoes inside temples or point your feet at people, elders, or deities.

QUESTIONS

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I wear to the wedding?

Festive and comfortable clothing is perfect. If you'd like to wear traditional Indian clothing, we'd love that too!

Is January hot in Madurai?

Yes, January is considerably warmer than winter in France. Bring lightweight clothing, sunscreen and sunglasses.

Do I need to know Tamil?

Absolutely not! English is widely used in many tourist and urban settings. And we'll teach you a few Tamil words anyway. ❤️

What about spicy food?

Not everything is spicy. If you're sensitive to spice, simply ask for less spicy food.
